Accuracy and Error Budget

This page is the scientific credential of the toolbox. It lists every approximation the code makes, its measured magnitude, and the conditions under which it matters. It consolidates section A.3 of the handover document in full.

Read the first row before any other. For an absolute Earth-fixed ↔ inertial rotation, the error is set by the Earth orientation parameters and not by this toolbox: about 1.5–2 mm at the surface for an IERS C04 series. Every algorithmic term below is at least an order smaller. The toolbox is built so that it will not become the limiting factor when the EOP improve, which is why tolerances in its test suite assert algorithmic agreement and never EOP-level agreement.

A measurement lesson worth carrying. An earlier revision recorded the UTM truncation as 0.06 mm. That was the easting alone, swept at a fixed offset from the central meridian, and it could not see the meridian arc term because that term does not vary with the quantity being swept. A one-way comparison at one offset is not a measurement of a truncation.

Attitude and navigation (L4b, L5)

QuantityMeasuredNotes
SLERP against held-out real GRACE-FO attitude 4.4e-6 rad median at 2 s gaps, 7.7e-6 at 30 s, against JPL's 8.8e-7 rad formal error Interpolation, not the algebra, is the limit at 30 s.
Frame sense against real flight data SRF axes at 1.20, 1.61, 0.99 deg from along-track, cross-track and radial over one orbit The residual is a real spacecraft's attitude control deadband, not numerical error. Recovered inclination 88.87 deg against a published 89.0.
Euler sequences and quaternion products against NAIF SPICE 1e-14 over 9600 comparisons Twelve sequences x two rotation types x two senses. The first check in the project that does not run against the toolbox's own transcription.
Attitude integration, coning case With exact rate quadrature the coning correction drives the residual to machine precision. In practice the limit is the trapezoidal reconstruction of ω between samples, so the measured benefit of the coning term is a factor of 2, not hundreds. A rate interface cannot do better; an integrated-increment (delta-theta) input would remove the dominant term. Deferred as P27.

The limit of every self-test in the rotation algebra. A wrong Euler sense, quaternion order or product still yields an orthogonal, unit-determinant matrix that round-trips through its own inverse. No self-test can catch a consistently wrong toolbox. The conventions here are therefore checked against independent authorities — NAIF SPICE, scipy, hand-computed literals and real GRACE-FO flight data — and the convention struct travels with the data so that a mismatch between two objects is detectable. That is a statement about what has been checked, not a proof of correctness.

Partial derivatives with respect to the EOP

Measured cost, N = 500 on one host: the three rotation partials are 1.15× the rotation itself and 4.00× with the rate partials, so they reuse the rotation's factors rather than re-deriving the chain. earthOrientationPartials, given a rotation you already hold, is eleven times cheaper than that rotation.

QuantityMeasuredVerdict
Constant-axis approximation in the generators neglects terms of order xp, yp, s′ (< 1e-6 rad) Exact to one part in 1e6 of themselves — six orders better than any use a partial is put to.
∂R/∂xp, ∂R/∂yp against a differenced rotation 4.8e-9 and 1.9e-9 At the central difference's own roundoff floor.
∂R/∂ΔUT1 against a differenced rotation 5.4e-10 at a 0.1 s perturbation, rising as 1/δ to 1.7e-7 at 1 ms Resolved: this is the oracle's roundoff floor, not the accuracy of the partial. Swept over δ = 1e-1…1e-5 s; the product (error × δ) has no trend, scattering about 4.3e-11, which corresponds to about 28 ulp of roundoff in the differenced rotation. A δ-independent error would not have fallen by 306× between 1 ms and 0.1 s. The sweep is bit-reproducible across runs, so its scatter is the exact rounding this chain produces at each perturbation and not the noise of the instrument. The analytic partials are closed-form products of the same factors the rotation is built from and are exact to roundoff; 1.7e-7 measures how well a finite difference can see them, which is a statement about the instrument.
Units per radian, not per arcsecond info.polarMotionUnits states it on every call. A factor of 206265 in a Jacobian is not visible in the answer.
Partials of Ṙ̇— Not provided, by decision D105. Ṙ̇ is a force-model quantity; no observation in scope is a function of it. Recorded so it is clear the question was answered rather than overlooked.

What is deliberately not in the toolbox

  • IAU 2000B as a fast nutation alternative: 1 mas ≈ 3 cm is an order worse than the EOP error floor, so it is unusable here (D16).
  • A bundled geoid grid, for licensing and size reasons (risk R8).
  • WGCCRE Tables 2 and 3 under Source = "iau"; they carry a different pole convention and route through the PCK instead (D100).
  • Partials of Ṙ̇ with respect to the EOP (D105).
  • A GCRS frame option on geocentricBodyState; the expansion is valid only near the Earth (D96).

The three items listed here in v2.0 — interpolated CIP mode, EOP partials, and barycentric frames and ephemerides — have all since been delivered, and are documented above and on the ephemerides page.
