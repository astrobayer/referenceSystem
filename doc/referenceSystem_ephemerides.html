Ephemerides, Body Frames and EOP Partials

These three layers arrived after the v2.0 pages were written, and until now Contents.m listed functions the browsable help did not describe. This page covers them: L5e the solar-system ephemeris and the BCRS ↔ GCRS transformation, L5f the planetary body-fixed frames, and the partial derivatives of the Earth rotation with respect to the EOP.

Read this first if you are here for an absolute position. The bundled ephemeris provider is analytic and is good to kilometres, not millimetres. Everything on this page that quotes a millimetre — the BCRS transformation in particular — is millimetre-accurate as a correction, applied to a position whose own error is four orders larger. The two must not be combined and then quoted at the smaller number.

L5e — two ephemeris tiers, and which one you have

barycentricBodyState and geocentricBodyState take a Source option with two values. They are not two speeds of the same thing; they are different theories with different error budgets, and info.positionError carries the figure for the body actually asked for on every call, so it never has to be looked up.

What the "mice" tier has actually been checked against

Four bodies — Sun, Moon, Mars barycentre, Jupiter barycentre — at three epochs, against a committed JPL HORIZONS fixture retrieved 2026-07-29. HORIZONS serves DE441 while the bundled kernel is DE440, so the test measures that difference rather than assuming it away.

The small-body path has never been executed against an external authority, and separately, on the shipped de440s.bsp the generation-pairing check of decision D15 was found at run 68 to parse no generation at all. Read ephemeris.meta.generationVerified: when it is false, meta.ephemeris is your own declaration and not something the files confirmed.

Ceres is in the HORIZONS fixture and is skipped, because it needs sb441-n16.bsp, which is not in the "orientation" kernel set. The skip is now reported in the test log rather than passed over in silence. Fetch the "smallBodies" set if you intend to rely on it, and treat the first result as a measurement rather than a confirmation.

Mixing kernel generations is warned about, never refused

readEphemeris reads each SPK's own comment area and reports every planetary ephemeris the furnished set names. Holding several generations for testing and comparison is ordinary work, so the toolbox's duty is to make sure you cannot mix them unknowingly, not to decide for you (decision D110).

  • meta.generations lists every generation found.
  • meta.ephemeris names the set — DE440+DE441 — rather than picking one of it.
  • A warning fires on every mixed call until you declare the set.
  • 'AllowGenerations', ["DE440" "DE441"] silences it for exactly that set. It takes a list and not a flag, because a flag says only “be quiet” and leaves no record of what was asserted.

When is a mixture right? DE440 and DE441 were fit to the same data set and differ in one modelling choice; the planets differ by under 1 m over DE440's span and the Moon by under 2 m across 1970–2020 (Park et al. 2021). Different fits are another matter: the barycentre moved about 100 km between DE430 and DE440, mainly from the added Kuiper-belt masses. SPICE resolves an overlapping body from whichever kernel was furnished last, so an unintended mixture is silent in the result.

One thing is still an error: an ExpectedEphemeris that none of the furnished files supports. That is not the toolbox overruling you — it is answering the question you asked it to ask.

L5e — the BCRS transformation

gcrsToBcrs and bcrsToGcrs implement IERS Conventions (2010) Eqs. (11.18) and (11.19) — not Chapter 10, which is time coordinates and contains no spatial transformation. The Conventions state their own uncertainty for the formula as below 1 mm, and that is the accuracy of these functions.

Scale has no default. The two choices differ by 9.5 cm against a formula uncertainty of 1 mm — ninety-five times — and neither is visible in the result, so the caller states which one they mean.

The ephemeris barely matters here, which is counter-intuitive. U and V reach the answer only through 1/c² terms, so their errors are suppressed by 1e-8: the bundled provider's 2.8 mm/s on the Earth's velocity moves the result by 4.8e-9 m. Do not reach for MICE on account of this function.

Velocity is transformed, from IAU 2000 Resolution B1.3, and its dT/dt factor is 1.5e-8 — 4.7e-4 m/s on 31 km/s. Validated by central difference at six step sizes at order 2.00 throughout, so the residual is the difference's own truncation and not a formula error.

geocentricBodyState returns a coordinate difference in the BCRS, not a GCRS state, and the two differ by about 6 m at the Moon. There is no 'Frame' option by decision (D94/D96): the BCRS-to-GCRS expansion is valid only near the Earth, and applying it to Jupiter's geocentric vector would give a smooth, plausible, wrong answer.

L5f — planetary body-fixed frames

bodyRotationElements returns α0, δ0 and W from the IAU WGCCRE 2015 report; rotationIcrfToBodyFixed assembles them into a rotation.

The two sources have different domains, not merely different accuracies. Source = "iau" covers Table 1 only — the Sun and the eight planets. Satellites, dwarf planets and minor planets (Phobos, Io, Titan, Ceres, Vesta …) resolve only through Source = "pck". Tables 2 and 3 are deliberately not transcribed, because section 6 of the report uses the opposite pole convention for dwarf planets, minor planets and comets, and folding them in would put two conventions behind one argument value (decision D100).

The report's own accuracy statement is one tenth of a degree, with Mercury, Mars, Saturn and Uranus carrying more digits. At Uranus the report notes that the present uncertainty of the prime meridian exceeds a complete rotation.

The pole is the one north of the invariable plane, which is not the right-hand-rule spin pole for a retrograde rotator: for Venus and Uranus the two differ by 180°. info.poleDefinition says so on every call and info.sense gives the sense of rotation from the sign of Ẇ.

The Earth and the Moon are absent on purpose. The 2015 report removed them; for the Earth use the rigorous chain (rotationItrsToGcrs) and for the Moon use a frame from the ephemeris, at any precision.

Partial derivatives with respect to the EOP

rotationItrsToGcrsPartials returns ∂R/∂xp, ∂R/∂yp and ∂R/∂ΔUT1, and — when five or more outputs are requested — the same three for Ṙ. earthOrientationPartials is the generator layer underneath and takes a rotation you already hold.

The partials are per radian, not per arcsecond. Polar motion is published in arcseconds and ΔUT1 in seconds; xp and yp reach these functions in radians, as everywhere else in the toolbox. A caller building a normal matrix from an arcsecond-parameterised estimate must scale by iersConstants().arcsecondsToRadians. info.polarMotionUnits states this on every call, because a factor of 206265 in a Jacobian is not visible in the answer — it just makes the estimate converge to the wrong place.

PropertyValue
Rotation partials Closed form: the same product chain with one elementary rotation replaced by its derivative. Exact to roundoff.
Constant-axis approximation Neglects terms of order xp, yp, s′ (< 1e-6 rad), so the partials are exact to about one part in 1e6 of themselves — six orders better than any use.
Rate partials D102 ordering: differentiate in the parameter first, difference in time second, with the same step rotationItrsToGcrs uses for Ṙ. info.rateConstruction records which order was taken, because the two are not interchangeable and a reader cannot tell from the numbers.
Agreement with a differenced rotation 4.8e-9 (xp), 1.9e-9 (yp), 1.7e-7 (ΔUT1) at the perturbations used. All three are the difference's own roundoff floor, not the accuracy of the partials; the ΔUT1 figure was swept over four decades of perturbation to establish that, and falls to 5.4e-10 at a 0.1 s step.
Partials of Ṙ̇ Not provided, by decision D105. Ṙ̇ exists for a force model, not for an estimator, and no observation in scope is a function of it.
